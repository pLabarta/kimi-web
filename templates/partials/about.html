About me

Apparently, I’m kind a cliche regarding the following quote from the book of the 33⅓ Main Series, “#163 Computer World” by Steve Tupai Francis.

As a designer, I identify with the philosophy of the Bauhaus movement. I found myself surrounded by pure geometric forms and primary colors. Yellow is my favorite color, Kraftwerk is my favorite music project, Computer Liebe is my favorite record, Nosferatu and Metropolis are my favorite films. My ability to organize, keep things simple, and prioritize has taken me to professionalize myself in production management. After completing my Bachelor’s degree in Product Design, I have specialized in Sociology and Digital Technologies. I actively seek opportunities to stay informed and updated about Internet technical standards and policy body discussions while advocating the Free and Open Source software culture. As a person committed to leveraging technology for social impact, I am deeply dedicated to building a global human and digital rights ecosystem, fostering conversations around minorities and underrepresented groups to promote a strengthened civil society, and empowering the digital resilience and integrity of civic space worldwide. I am committed to exploring different forms of Internet and technological approaches, human-focused technologies, understanding the impact of censorship, surveillance, and disinformation and how users and communities address the challenges encountered during that process in multiple world regions, especially in South America. Within activist colleagues and community networks around the world, I contribute to the establishment of technical and human standards and policies, both online and offline, that enhance resilience to censorship and bolster communications security for independent and civil organizations and citizens, amplifying the voices of marginalized groups in governance, online and in the territory, with an intersectional approach that recognizes that threats to different ways of living are compounded by the intersection of human and digital rights abuses or discrimination based on ethnicity, religion, gender, sexuality, social class, or other means of marginalization.

As part of the LATAM/LGBTIQ+ community based in Buenos Aires, I collaborate with different artistic collectives, music projects, and the entertainment industry producing live music shows and audiovisual performances.

I identify myself as a non-binary person regarding my indigenous ancestry, like many colleagues in the region. That is why my workspaces, collaborations, and activism are crossed by my identity and heritage. I believe that as an organized civil society, we are at a turning point in which we have the possibilities and the means to speak with our voice to enhance the voices of our peers, to recover the acknowledgment of those who came before us, and to serve as a platform to raise the voices of future generations.
